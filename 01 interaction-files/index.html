Classroom Demographic Description

Students will base their cumulative final project on the following preschool classroom scenario. For this final culminating assignment, you will imagine you are the lead teacher in the ABC Preschool Classroom.  You are responsible for preparing a lesson that incorporates the principles and strategies you have learned in order to meet the learning needs of the children in the classroom.

The ABC Pre-K Classroom is a public school inclusive preschool serving children ages 4 to 5 years old. The class meets five days a week for a full school day with 18 children enrolled.

The Teaching Team
  • one lead teacher,
  • one teaching assistant, and
  • access to related service providers including a speech-language pathologist, occupational therapist, and special education support staff as needed.
Curriculum & Climate

The classroom follows Connect for Learning, a developmentally appropriate, play-based, language-rich curriculum with embedded whole-group, small-group, and center-based instruction. The teacher uses read-alouds, songs, movement, visual supports, hands-on materials, and learning centers to support engagement and early literacy development.

The classroom climate emphasizes inclusion, peer interaction, communication supports, and active participation for all children. The teachers in the ABC Inclusive Pre-K Classroom hold values in keeping children meaningfully engaged with peers and embedding supports into regular classroom routines whenever possible. The teachers use their knowledge of universal design for learning principles as well as use differentiation and individualization to ensure every child in their classroom has the support they need to succeed.

The Students

The classroom is composed of 18 children from a range of family structures and community backgrounds. Several children live in multigenerational households, and some families report limited access to books and literacy materials in the home. Families speak multiple languages, including English, Spanish and Arabic.

  • 13 children are primarily English-speaking.
  • 4 children are dual language learners.
  • 1 child has been diagnosed with Autism Spectrum Disorder and speech-language impairment.

The class reflects a broad range of developmental abilities. Most children demonstrate age-expected growth in social-emotional development, oral language, and early literacy; however, the group includes children with varying levels of readiness in:

  • expressive and receptive language,
  • print awareness,
  • alphabet knowledge,
  • phonological awareness,
  • attention and self-regulation,

Mateo’s expressive communication challenges and need for predictable structure affect his ability to participate independently in whole-group literacy activities.
